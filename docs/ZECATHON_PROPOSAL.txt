Centralized Agro Hub (CAHS)
A connected digital platform for agricultural trade, storage, logistics, payments and responsible finance
Category: Agricultural Technology · Digital Finance · AI · Supply Chain Management
Executive Summary
CAHS is a digital agricultural ecosystem that brings production, produce trading, storage, transportation, payments and eligible financing onto one platform. It connects farmers, buyers and traders, storage and cold-room operators, verified logistics companies and participating financial institutions. By organising these activities in a single hub, CAHS aims to reduce supply-chain fragmentation, improve transaction visibility, reduce avoidable spoilage and delivery uncertainty, and help eligible farmers and agricultural businesses access appropriate financial services, while creating measurable business opportunities for Zenith Bank. CAHS also adds three AI-enabled capabilities: an assistant that resolves common customer complaints step by step and routes the rest to the bank by urgency; a pending-state payment engine that retries incomplete transfers automatically while the customer sees a single transaction; and AI financial insights that turn recorded money-in and money-out into revenue and expenditure breakdowns, growth tracking and a business-improvement plan.

AI Customer Support & Complaint Resolution
Customers describe a problem in their own words (text, and voice or local-language support where available). The assistant works in two stages, so simple problems are fixed in minutes and complex ones reach the bank already organised.
Stage 1: Guided self-service
Identifies the issue type: payment problem, delivery delay, order dispute, loan application status, refund request, account or verification issue.
Checks the relevant order, payment or shipment status inside the platform and explains what it shows.
Gives clear, numbered steps for the customer to follow, then confirms whether the problem is resolved.
Never asks for a PIN, OTP or password, and tells customers never to share them.
Stage 2: Urgency-ranked escalation to the bank
If the assistant cannot solve the issue, it opens a case with a plain-language summary, the transaction reference, a timeline of events and the conversation so far, so the customer does not have to repeat themselves. Cases are ranked automatically:
Priority	Typical examples	Handling
Critical	Money debited or held with no confirmation; suspected fraud or unauthorised transaction.	Top of the queue; immediate alert to the bank team.
High	Perishable produce delivery at risk; failed loan disbursement or repayment problem; repeated unresolved contact.	Reviewed ahead of routine cases.
Medium	Order or quality disputes; document verification delays.	Handled in normal order.
Low	General enquiries, feedback, how-to questions.	Handled in normal order; often answered by the assistant.

Ranking considers the amount of money at risk, how time-sensitive the goods are (for example perishables), whether fraud indicators are present, and how long the issue has been open. The bank sees a live dashboard of cases by priority, status and owner, and customers see status updates on their own complaint.
Response-time targets and priority rules are to be agreed with Zenith Bank. The assistant does not reverse payments, approve refunds or make final decisions; those stay with authorised bank staff. Fraud-related and sensitive cases are always escalated to a person.
Pending-State Payment Protection
When a transfer does not complete first time, the customer is not left with a failed or double-debited payment. The transaction stays in a pending state while CAHS retries it automatically, and the customer sees one transaction, not repeated attempts.
Step	What happens
1. Customer pays	A single transaction is created with a unique reference. The customer sees "Processing".
2. Attempt does not complete	Status changes to "Pending". The customer sees no failed attempt and no extra entries.
3. Automatic retries	CAHS retries in the background, with increasing intervals, using the same reference so a retry can never create a second debit.
4a. Success	Status becomes "Successful". The debit is confirmed, and the seller and order are updated.
4b. Retry window ends	Status becomes "Not completed". No final debit is taken, any hold on the customer's funds is released, and the customer is notified with an option to try again.

Funds are finally debited only once the transfer is confirmed.
Every attempt is recorded internally with a timestamp for the bank, reconciliation and dispute handling, but is hidden from the customer-facing view.
The seller is paid and the order released only after confirmed success.
Cases that stay pending beyond a set time are escalated automatically through the complaint triage above.
Retry rules, the retry window, handling of customer funds while pending, and reversal timelines require Zenith Bank and payment-partner approval and must follow applicable CBN and payment-scheme rules. A time limit is proposed so that stale payments are not completed long after the customer expects.
